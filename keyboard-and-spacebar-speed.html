Guide

Does your keyboard change your spacebar score?

Yes, more than most people think. The same person can score a full CPS higher on one keyboard than another. Here is why.

Updated October 23, 2026

Key travel and actuation

Every key has two important distances. Travel is how far it can move from top to bottom. Actuation is how far you have to push it before the keyboard registers a press. The shorter and lighter the actuation, the less work each tap takes, and the faster you can repeat it.

The three common keyboard types

TypeFeelEffect on spacebar tests
Laptop (scissor)Short, shallow and fairly light.Good for fast tapping because travel is short. The large spacebar can feel uneven at the far ends.
MembraneSoft and a little mushy, with a rubber dome under each key.Usually the slowest. You need to push further before the dome collapses, and the return is slower.
MechanicalA separate switch under each key, with a clear actuation point.Light linear switches are often the fastest of all. Heavy or clicky switches can be slower than a laptop.

Stabilisers

A spacebar is so wide that it needs metal wires called stabilisers to keep it level. If they are loose, dry or badly fitted, the bar can rattle, tilt or stick slightly when pressed off-centre. That slows you down and can make presses feel inconsistent. Pressing near the middle of the bar avoids most of the problem.

Chatter and double counts

Some switches, especially old or cheap ones, send two signals for one press. This is called chatter. It makes a counter jump by two and can inflate your score. Keyboards try to filter it with a short delay called debounce. If your count rises faster than you are tapping, chatter is the likely cause.

Wireless keyboards and lag

Bluetooth and wireless keyboards add a small delay to each press. Because the delay is the same for every press, it barely changes a CPS score. What can matter is a low battery or interference, which can drop presses entirely. If your score suddenly falls on a wireless keyboard, try a fresh battery or a cable.

Touch screens

On a phone or tablet the on-screen key counts each tap. Touch screens have no travel at all, so many people score higher on a phone than on a keyboard, especially with two thumbs. Treat phone and keyboard scores as separate records.

The fairest comparison

If you are competing with friends, use the same keyboard and the same test length. If you are tracking your own progress, stick to one device so improvements reflect your hands and not your hardware. You can do both on the Spacebar Clicker and the Spacebar Counter.
