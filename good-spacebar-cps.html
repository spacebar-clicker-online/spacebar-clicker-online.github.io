What is a good spacebar CPS?

A good score depends on how long the test was. Here is how CPS is worked out and the ranges to compare yourself with.

Updated October 23, 2026

How CPS is calculated

CPS means clicks per second. For a spacebar test it is the number of separate presses divided by the length of the test in seconds.

Example: 72 presses in a 10-second test is 72 ÷ 10 = 7.2 CPS.

A held key does not count as many presses. Good testers, including ours, ignore the repeated signal a keyboard sends while a key stays down.

Score ranges by test length

These are rough guides for one-thumb or two-thumb tapping on a normal keyboard. Your own numbers will vary with your keyboard, your hand and how warmed up you are.

1 secondunder 56–89–1213+
5 secondsunder 55–78–1112+

Why longer tests give lower scores

Your thumb runs on short bursts of energy. For the first few seconds it can move at its top speed, then the muscles start to tire and your rhythm slips. A 1-second test only measures the burst, while a 60-second test is mostly about how well you hold up. That is why it only makes sense to compare scores from the same test length. Our result card keeps a separate best for each mode for this reason.

Average CPS vs best second

The Spacebar Clicker shows two speed numbers. Average CPS covers the whole run. Best 1 second is the most presses you made inside any single second. If your best second is much higher than your average, you have the speed but not the stamina, and pacing practice will help. If they are close, you are already steady and need to work on raw speed.

Comparing fairly with friends

  • Use the same test length. The 10-second mode is the usual standard.
  • Use the same device if you can. Keyboards differ more than people expect.
  • Take the best of three runs rather than a single attempt. Short tests swing a lot.
  • Agree on one or two thumbs before you start.
